An engine, and one world running on it

Give your audience something to build on.

Inspiral runs the space where they do it. Every contribution is a change to somebody else's. A Mind writes the line that says what the change was, and the log it goes into refuses UPDATE and DELETE, so nobody can be edited off their own work.

How it works

A move is a set of choices, not an essay.

A piece declares slots. Each slot offers a finite list, written by the creator out of their own material, so the palette is the IP. One line of prose survives — the caption, why you did it. Picking is not writing, and a blank textarea is the reason most people never contribute anything.

Main required
fennel leek celeriac
Method required
braise raw roast
Finish
lemon vinegar none

Ada's move. A value the schema does not offer is dropped on write.

You build on somebody by changing one thing.

Everything is an extension of something. The creator seeds the piece, the first visitor extends the seed, everyone after extends either. One root, no separate "add" action, no special case for the first move — which is why somebody built on my thing is one exact query rather than a heuristic.

Maya extends Ada and changes one slot. Because both moves are sets of values, the difference is computed, not inferred: 

KEPT Main: fennel, Finish: lemon
CHANGED Method: braise -> raw

The Mind writes one sentence.

It is handed that diff and asked to write it well — not handed two texts and asked to spot the difference. It names one thing kept and one thing changed, in the contributors' own words, addressed to the person waiting.

Written by the Mind

Maya kept your fennel and bread but changed your hour-long braise into a raw mandoline shave salted hard until it weeps, with the bread toasted in the butter instead.

That one came from two paragraphs of prose, on a piece with no slots — the weaker path, and still the fallback. If the Mind is unavailable the sentence is absent and the contribution stands. Losing the narration must never lose the work.

The log refuses UPDATE and DELETE.

A piece is a row. An extension is an event, and never a row. Attribution is the product, so it is held by the database rather than by application code: 

CREATE TRIGGER IF NOT EXISTS events_no_update
BEFORE UPDATE ON events
BEGIN
  SELECT RAISE(ABORT, 'events is append-only: UPDATE is forbidden');
END;

A takedown is therefore additive: a new event later readers respect. The work disappears from the lineage, the public page, the permalink and the return screen. The history stays intact underneath.

The example world

A kitchen, because a recipe is the most naturally extendable thing there is: everybody already understands that you can keep somebody's base and change their acid. Disagreement is native to it. Five pieces, and it opens with arguments already in them — nobody extends a blank page.

Five Ingredients

The brief, in the creator's words

Five ordinary things, one dish worth arguing about. Say what you would do and why. Disagreeing with what is already here is the point, not a problem.

Four takes deep. The last one branches off the first instead of the newest, because a lineage is a tree and an argument is allowed to fork.

The kitchen's pieces are free text, not slots. Both shapes work and neither replaces the other — a piece with an empty schema takes prose, and the Mind compares the two texts. The palette above is the stronger path and it is not what this world runs on.

What this is not

  • Identity is asserted, not authenticated. A fan id is a string the client supplies. Clear your storage and you are a stranger; copy somebody's id and you are them. There is a claim-and-verify flow that proves you control a delivery address and makes an id yours — it verifies an address, never a person, and nothing with money behind it should rest on it.
  • The retention thesis is untested. The whole bet is that being told what somebody changed about your work is what brings you back. Half of returning people get the sentence, half get a generic line: that A/B is runnable and has not been run. Nobody knows whether this works.
  • Slot palettes are declared in code. POST /v1/pieces takes a title, a brief and a location. Writing the slots a piece is made of is a function call today, not an endpoint, and no creator-facing tool builds one.
  • The app is a reference implementation. It exists to prove the API is enough to build against, not to be looked at.

Built on Animoca Minds. The Mind is the host: it routes visitors, writes what changed, and decides what reaches the creator.
